# Business Problem

Female workforce participation is a common headline measure of gender
equity. But participation only measures who is in the workforce. It says
nothing about whether that presence converts into management roles —
the leadership pipeline.

A country can report strong participation and still have a pipeline that
stalls before management. If the two move together, participation is a
fair proxy and tracking it is enough. If they are decoupled, the headline
measure hides the problem: getting more women into the workforce will
not, on its own, change who holds management roles.

This project measures that gap across 190 countries using public
ILOSTAT and World Bank data, with a single metric:

**Conversion ratio = female manager share ÷ female employment share.**

A ratio below 1.0 means women hold management roles at a lower rate than
their presence in the workforce would predict.

**The question:** does high female workforce participation convert into
proportional management representation — and where is the gap widest?

**The decision it informs:** whether participation is enough as a
measure of progress, or whether conversion into management has to be
tracked separately. The same check applies inside an organisation:
representation by headcount versus representation in management.
